Sirsasana

Supported Headstand

InversionAdvanced
⏱Hold 30 seconds to 5 minutes
CoreShouldersArmsSpine

Overview

Headstand is known as the 'king of asanas' in traditional yoga. It reverses the flow of gravity, energizing the body and calming the mind. It requires significant core and shoulder strength and should be built up to gradually with proper preparation.

  • ✓Increases blood flow to the brain, improving focus and clarity
  • ✓Strengthens the shoulders, arms, and core
  • ✓Stimulates the pituitary and pineal glands
  • ✓Relieves stress and mild depression
  • ✓Develops balance, concentration, and inner calm

How to do it

  1. 1

    Kneel and interlace your fingers, placing your forearms on the mat shoulder-width apart.

  2. 2

    Place the crown of your head on the mat, cradled by your cupped hands.

  3. 3

    Tuck your toes and lift your hips, walking your feet in toward your elbows.

  4. 4

    Engage your core and slowly lift one knee into your chest, then the other.

  5. 5

    Press firmly into your forearms — 80% of your weight should be in the arms, not the head.

  6. 6

    Straighten your legs upward, squeezing them together, and breathe steadily.

  7. 7

    To come down, lower one leg at a time with control. Rest in Child's Pose for 30 seconds.

⚠What to avoid

  • Practicing without shoulder and core strength built up first

  • Putting most of your weight on your head and neck — arms bear the load

  • Kicking up forcefully — float up with core control

  • Practicing during menstruation, with neck injuries, high blood pressure, or glaucoma

Pro tips

💡

Build up to this pose with Dolphin Pose and forearm plank to develop the necessary strength.

💡

Practice near a wall until you develop confidence and balance.

💡

The prep pose (Dolphin) is itself an excellent inversion — don't rush the full pose.
