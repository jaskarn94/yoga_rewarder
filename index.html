Adho Mukha Svanasana

Downward-Facing Dog

StandingBeginner
⏱Hold 1–3 minutes
HamstringsCalvesShouldersSpineCore

Overview

Downward-Facing Dog is one of yoga's most recognized poses and a cornerstone of most practices. It simultaneously stretches the back body while strengthening the arms and shoulders, serving as both a resting pose and an active inversion.

  • ✓Lengthens the hamstrings, calves, and spine
  • ✓Strengthens the arms, shoulders, and wrists
  • ✓Energizes the body and calms the nervous system
  • ✓Improves circulation via gentle inversion
  • ✓Relieves headaches, fatigue, and mild back pain

How to do it

  1. 1

    Start on hands and knees (tabletop). Place wrists under shoulders and knees under hips.

  2. 2

    Spread your fingers wide and press through every knuckle.

  3. 3

    Tuck your toes and exhale as you lift your hips up and back, forming an inverted V.

  4. 4

    Straighten your legs as much as possible — a small bend in the knees is fine.

  5. 5

    Press the floor away with your hands and lengthen through the entire spine.

  6. 6

    Let your head hang between your arms, ears in line with your biceps.

  7. 7

    Press your heels toward the floor (they need not touch). Hold for 1–3 minutes.

⚠What to avoid

  • Rounding your upper back — externally rotate the upper arms to open your shoulders

  • Placing too much weight in your wrists — distribute weight evenly across the hands

  • Letting your lower back round — bend your knees to maintain a long spine

  • Collapsing your chest toward the floor — keep the spine actively lengthened

Pro tips

💡

Think 'hips high, heels low' — prioritize a long spine over straight legs.

💡

Pedal your feet alternately to loosen the calves and hamstrings before settling in.

💡

Engage your core lightly to prevent your lower back from overextending.
